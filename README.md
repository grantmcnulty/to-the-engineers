# To the engineers

*A pact from a sales leader who has spent almost 30 years on your side of the table.*

Somewhere on GitHub right now, someone is building something remarkable. It works. It solves a real problem. And it will probably never reach the people who would pay for it, because the person who built it would rather keep building than sell, and doesn't trust the people who sell for a living.

From my perspective, that distrust has been earned.

## What has gone wrong

Most engineers I know have a story. A salesperson promised a customer a feature that didn't exist, then handed the deadline to the team. A deal closed on a demo that only worked on one laptop. A quarter was celebrated on stage, and the people who built the product weren't mentioned. The commission was large; the understanding of the technology was small; the thanks never came.

That isn't every salesperson. But it has happened often enough that, when someone in sales asks to talk about your invention, the sensible response is to keep looking down and pray they move away.

I've spent my career in enterprise software, at SAP, TIBCO, Cisco, VCE, Axonify and in early start-ups, and I've seen both sides of this. The best results I have ever been part of came from one thing: the people selling and the people building treating each other as one team, with the same facts, the same goals and a fair share of what it earned.

## How I have worked

I've always sat as close to the engineers as they would let me.

At Cisco I worked in the Advanced Technologies Group, taking the newest products to their first customers, where you can't sell anything you don't understand because nobody else has used it yet. I was on the founding team at VCE, the joint venture between Cisco, EMC and VMware, winning some of its earliest customers alongside the people building it. Earlier, at a start-up called Amacis, I sold a capability our engineers had built for SMS and MMS to Orange, and that work was pivotal in the company being acquired.

When the Amacis deal paid out, it was the largest commission cheque I had ever earned. I spent the next three days writing a handwritten note to every engineer on the team, explaining that it could never have happened without them, and I shared the commission with everyone on that team.

Today I build with AI every day myself, with Claude and GPT, including my own Personal OS and several applications that could become viable products. Now I have time to consider all my options, and this call to engineers has been on my mind for many years; I'm finally in the right place to share it. I'm not an engineer, and I won't pretend to be. But I know what it takes to make something work, and I respect the people who do it well.

## How I build

I work in Claude Code most days, with a small team of AI agents doing the legwork. MCP servers give them tools, including one that fact-checks claims against their sources and grades them before anything goes out. Scheduled agents brief me each morning, look ahead each week and run nightly quality checks. My website, CV and booking system run from a single content source, deployed on Cloudflare, with everything under version control. It isn't engineering at your level, but it means I understand how things get built, and I can talk about your work with the people who will buy it without overclaiming.

## The pact

If we work together, these are the terms I hold myself to.

1. **The builders come first.** Your name goes on what you built: in every deck, every customer conversation and every announcement.
2. **I understand it before I sell it.** I learn how your product works, not just what it does. If I can't explain it accurately to a potential buyer, I don't pitch it.
3. **I never sell what isn't built.** No promises to customers that you haven't agreed to deliver.
4. **Customer truth comes back unfiltered.** What buyers actually said, including the parts that sting, so you can build the right thing.
5. **Rewards are agreed up front, in writing.** Everyone shares in proportion to what they bring, the split is visible to everyone involved, and we review it openly if the contribution changes. No hidden terms.
6. **Your invention stays yours.** I help you take it to market. I don't take ownership of what you built.
7. **Either of us can walk away cleanly,** on terms we agreed at the start.

## What I'm looking for

Something real that already works, even if it's rough. A builder who wants it used, not just admired. And an honest conversation about whether there's a business in it, including the possibility that there isn't yet.
